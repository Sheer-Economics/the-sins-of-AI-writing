# The Sins of Writing with AI

In a couple of days, I stumble on two discrete articles provoking debates
on sins of writing with AI. 

The first is a [report](https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/books/features/jerry-falade-book-deal-ai-racism-b3050738.html) 
of a fictional writer who allegedly lose a 2-million-dollar deal due to 
publishers have no full faith in ruling out AI writing. 

The second artile is a plea to not writing with AI substantively from [Hacker
News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49767937). The author listed three
main reasons: 

> (1) the writing process is an essential part of the thinking process,
> (2) AI writing is vague and wrong in hard-to-notice ways, and
> (3) writing with AI is rude and misleading.

I actually agree with them, only to argue that those reasons won't logically
deduce the conclusion agaisnt AI.

This is a growing contentious issue as AI is permeating our lives. I would
like to analyze it from an economics perspective.

## Who is the culprit

AI is the new buzz word and easily enters into the spotlight becoming the focus
  of criticism.
Is AI a tool or a species comparable to human?
For now, I see AI as a tool, a more powerful tool than any kind in human
  history.
Thus, AI won't take away human spirit.

Once replacing the buzzword "AI" with a "tool", it becomes more apparent that
  the culprit is never the tool but the human who uses it.
The whole technology history tells stories of all kinds of tool invention.
Almost every time, there had been double casted on the new tools. 

In 1966, math teachers protested against calculator use
      [^1](https://miroslao7.medium.com/chatgpt-wont-take-your-job-away-a96d9a7b6a8).
Current writers' protest against using AI in writing airs a nostalgia feeling.
After 1966, calculators are used more in education.
However, the debate is not conclusive.
With calculators adopted in schools, math has been advancing however math
  education results are not
  improving[^3](https://www.theaustraliatoday.com.au/australias-reading-and-maths-results-are-at-an-all-time-low-but-theres-more-to-the-story/).
They aimed at the wrong target. 

## Would protests stop the using of AI

People will alway try an economical way of doing businesses, such as cutting
  costs or improve products.
That's the source of economic growth.
Therefore, effort go against the trend of technology innovation will go in
  vain.
The usual strong pushback stems from people whose interest have been negatively
  impacted by the emerging technology.
It's much like the British [Red Flag
          Act](https://law-school.open.ac.uk/blog/red-flag-act) 1865, which required any
  self-propelled road vehicle to be preceded by a person walking at least 60
  yards ahead carrying a red flag.
This law was abolished in 1896.
Similar act had been passed in U.S in 1894 and only to be repealed two years
  later.
The resistance to the technology advancement is peril.
A better option, even for people whose livehood are disrupted, is to adopt it
  rather than resist it.
The adoption shall be the earlier the better because early adopters face less
  competition.

## Will the prevail of AI eliminating good writing

I agreed with the author on his three points: 1) Writing helps thinking; 2) AI
  writing is fraught of errors; 3) AI writing without disclosure is misleading.
Are those going to make good writings extinct?
Absolutely not.
The economics laws stipulate that the market will make good writing win.
Much similar to we can buy good quality milk without know how to make it.
If there are enough customers prefering genuine human writing, the prices of
  those good writing will rise.
If everything were robotic at an ecolog end of world, any spirit related
  writing would be as precious as water in a desert.
Thus, the flooding of AI writing is only a transitional noise.

Having said that, AI will cause a divergence of writings.
Some good writers will use the tool to improve their writing without loosing
  their uniqueness.
Others who rely too much of AI and drop their capacity of writing or thinking
  independently will be trapped. 

## The key question to ask

For people who learn to use tools well, writing with AI's assist can turn out to
  be more strenous than writing it without AI.
It would output a better writing because they use AI to stress test it. 
Many others who do not care about the result would output the "noise". 

The key question for individual writers is about how to stand out from the
  noice.
But for the market, it is about how to reduce the noise.

For example, more platform would require disclosing the use of AI.
Specific option for use of AI in reviews and rating.
Because of the increasing cost of telling whether AI writing is involved, as
  shown in Falade's case, publishers may opt to invitation-only authors.
As a result, more authors choose self-publishing, bearing cost and benefit of
  their own writing.
This will be a good constraint for authors not to abuse the AI in writing.

And there will be more innovative method to reduce noises if the market is
  allowed to function efficiently.
